### The "Restart"
For a long time, I was blindly sliding along a predefined "correct track," easily securing high scores through imitation. However, a deep disillusionment grew within me—I realized that much of contemporary AI research feels like "playing with Lego blocks"—aimlessly patching models and chasing benchmarks without a deep understanding of the underlying mechanisms.

Recently, I hit the **"Restart"** button. I am no longer content with being an imitator or a passive navigator. To put my philosophy into practice, I am currently diving into **Stanford University's CS336 (Language Modeling from Scratch)** to understand how to build complex systems entirely from the ground up. Inspired by the research philosophy of **Yang Xu**, I no longer want to just explain the past; I want to construct the future.

### Research Taste: What I Avoid
I have developed a sharp "compass" for the research I consciously distance myself from:
* **Unscalable Toy-Model Analysis**: I am skeptical of post-hoc analyses on small models that lack the capacity to *scale up*. In the era of scaling laws, conclusions from toy models are often too fragile to generalize to the frontiers of intelligence.
* **Agentic "Harness" Wrapping**: I have no interest in superficial engineering that merely wraps black-box models in tool-calling loops (similar to the philosophy behind some current agent harnesses). I find this kind of "wrapper-style" research creates an illusion of progress without contributing to the core. **I want to build the engine, not just wrap a shell around it.**

### Mission & Philosophy
